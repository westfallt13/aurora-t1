WHERE THE "RANDOM NUMBERS/SYMBOLS" ISSUE SHOWS UP
Going through samples/100M/tests/*.txt category by category, the collapse-into-symbols problem splits cleanly by prompt type:

- Open, prose-style prompts (creative writing, grammar) stay fluent for their whole length — no symbol collapse. Sentence-level grammar, spelling, punctuation are solid. Content drifts off-topic (creative writing wanders from "robot discovers music" into generic photo/video web-advice content) or is semantically confused (grammar's snowstorm/tornado mixup), but it reads like real English the whole way through — specifically, it reads like a FineWeb-Edu web article: long-form, second-person, "how to" register, regardless of what the prompt asked for.
- Prompts phrased as a direct question or a structured-output instruction (math, knowledge, following-instructions, conversation, and — after ~90 tokens — coding) are exactly where the collapse happens. Math ("=" repeated) and conversation ("l>" repeated) collapse almost immediately with no real content at all. Coding and following-instructions manage a sentence or two of vague, ungrounded prose before locking into a loop.

Working hypothesis: the model has picked up FineWeb-Edu's dominant long-form prose register reasonably well, but question/instruction-shaped prompts push it into territory it hasn't seen much of (FineWeb-Edu is mostly articles, not Q&A or instruction-following pairs) — once it's out of its comfort zone, the sampler falls into a low-entropy repetition trap. This is most likely a training-maturity issue rather than a decoding-settings one — not chasing the Modelfile angle further for now.
